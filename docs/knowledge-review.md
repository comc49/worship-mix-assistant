## The ten values I am least sure of

1. **Live-room low cut bump (+20 Hz on everything except kick and bass)** plus **+10 Hz for HVAC on open mics.** Stacked, a medium voice lands at 130 Hz and a low voice sits exactly on its 110 Hz limit.
2. **Vocal low-cut ceilings by voice range** (110 / 140 / 170 Hz). These decide when stacking stops; they are judgment calls.
3. **Wedge cap of 6 dB vocal gain reduction with no makeup gain.** From the brief; the right number depends on how loud your wedges run.
4. **Beginner electric guitar: ratio +6 (to 8:1) with a 5 ms attack.** "Limiter-style" as asked, but it is the most aggressive setting in the app.
5. **Keys low cut stack:** 60 Hz baseline, +30 beginner (bass present), +20 live room, +20 because a bass guitar is in the band = 130 Hz. Limit is 150 Hz.
6. **Electronic drums as a stereo pair: 2 dB of compression, capped at 3 dB.** Many engineers would use none.
7. **Reverb times:** plate 1.6 s, hall 2.2 s, drum room 0.8 s; live room shortens them to 1.2 / 1.6 / 0.6 s and caps the sends.
8. **Energy DCA members** (electronic drums, snare, toms, hi-hat, overheads, electric guitar) and **Low End DCA members** (kick, bass guitar, synth bass, cajon). Assumed: the brief left these brackets unfilled.
9. **Monitor starting levels** (own channel 0 dB, lead vocal -6 dB to everyone, keys -10 dB, electronic drums -8 dB) and which musicians share a mix.
10. **No gate on the snare.** The conservative choice; a gate is offered only under "if you hear".

Optional suggested cuts:
- Low-mid mud: -2 dB at 250 Hz, medium Q. Bass DIs often build up here and mask the vocal's body.

If you hear:
- Boomy, one note louder than the rest: First ask the player to play that note softer. Then cut 3 dB around 80 to 120 Hz, medium Q, sweeping to find it.

- Clacky string noise: Cut 2 to 3 dB around 2 to 3 kHz, medium Q.
- Bass and kick blur together: Decide which one owns 60 to 80 Hz. Cut the other one 2 to 3 dB there.

- Thin when the band drops out: Lower the low cut by 20 Hz for that song.

### Pad (`padSynth.di`)

Connections: stereoDi, monoDi. DCAs: keysAndPads. Group bus: keys. Sub send (own-bus sub): no.

| Setting | Default | Usable range | Musical limit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lowCutHz | 120 Hz | 80 to 200 | 80 to 250 (Above 250 Hz a pad loses its warmth entirely.) | A pad is atmosphere. Its low end only clouds the bass and the room. |
| levelDb | -5 dB | -12 to 0 | usable range | A pad should be felt more than heard. |
| monitorSendDb | -3 dB | -12 to 3 | usable range | The player needs to hear it, but pads fill a monitor quickly. |
| fxSend.hall | -22 dB | -30 to -14 | usable range | Pads already carry their own space. |

Gate: none. A direct source has no bleed from other instruments, so there is nothing for a gate to remove.

Compressor: none. Pads have no sudden peaks. A compressor would only bring up their level over time.

Optional suggested cuts:
- Low-mid cloud: -3 dB at 300 Hz, wide Q. Sustained pads stack up low-mids that cover everything else.

If you hear:
- The whole mix sounds cloudy: Pull the pad down 3 dB before touching anything else.
- Pad swells too loud: Ask the player to use their volume pedal, or ride the fader down.

### Lead vocal (`leadVocal.mic`)

Connections: dynamicMic, condenserMic. DCAs: leadVocal. Group bus: vocals. Sub send (own-bus sub): no.

| Setting | Default | Usable range | Musical limit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lowCutHz | 100 Hz | 80 to 140 | usable range | Removes handling noise, stage rumble and plosives below the voice. |
| compRatio | 3 :1 | 2 to 4 | 1.5 to 5 (Above 5:1 a lead vocal sounds squashed and lifts stage noise between phrases.) | A moderate ratio evens out phrases without sounding squashed. |
| compAttackMs | 15 ms | 10 to 30 | usable range | Slow enough to let consonants through so words stay clear. |
| compReleaseMs | 120 ms | 80 to 200 | usable range | Lets go between phrases without pumping. |
| compGainReductionDb | 4 dB | 3 to 6 | 2 to 8 (More than 8 dB of reduction on a vocal raises feedback risk and flattens the song.) | About 4 dB on loud phrases keeps the vocal on top without lifting stage noise much. |
| levelDb | 0 dB | -3 to 3 | usable range | The lead vocal is the reference. Everything else is balanced under it. |
| monitorSendDb | 0 dB | -10 to 5 | usable range | Singers need themselves first in their own monitor. |

| fxSend.tempoDelay | -20 dB | -30 to -12 | usable range | A quiet delay fills gaps between phrases on slower songs. |

Gate: none. A gate on a vocal chops quiet words and breaths. Mute the channel when the singer is not singing instead.

Compressor: yes, set by target gain reduction (never a threshold in dB)

If you hear:
- Muddy or woolly: Cut 2 to 3 dB around 200 to 300 Hz, medium Q. Check the mic is not touching the lips.
- Boxy, like singing in a cardboard box: Cut 2 to 4 dB around 300 to 500 Hz, medium Q.
- Nasal or honky: Cut 2 to 3 dB around 800 Hz to 1.2 kHz, medium Q.
- Harsh or piercing on loud notes: Cut 2 to 3 dB around 2.5 to 4 kHz, wide Q. Do not boost anything first.
- Sharp "s" and "t" sounds: Use the de-esser if one is loaded. Otherwise cut 2 dB around 6 to 8 kHz, medium Q.
- Thin, no body: Lower the low cut by 20 Hz first. Only then consider removing a cut around 200 to 300 Hz.
- Pops on "p" and "b": Raise the low cut by 20 Hz and ask the singer to aim the mic slightly off to the side of the mouth.
- Words get lost under the band: Before touching EQ: pull the keys and guitars DCAs down 2 dB. Then ride the vocal fader.

Effect times:
- vocalPlate: +0.4 s. A dry room gives no tail, so the plate provides it.
- hall: +0.4 s. A longer hall replaces the space the room lacks.
- drumRoom: +0.2 s. Drums sound small in a dry room.
- tempoDelay: +0 s. Delay time follows the song tempo, not the room.

Constant low-frequency noise (HVAC): +10 Hz low cut on every open mic except kick and cajon.

Precedence: offsets add in order (mic, player, room, band), then context limits clamp (room reverb ceilings, wedge cap), then the musical limit clamps last.

## Send effects

| Priority | Effect | Time | Limit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Vocal plate | 1.6 s (1 to 2.4) | 0.8 to 2.6 s | The one reverb every band needs: it gives the vocals sustain and blends harmonies. |
| 2 | Tempo delay | Tap the tempo of each song so the repeats land on the quarter note. Keep feedback low: two or three repeats. | - | A quiet delay on the lead vocal fills gaps without the wash of more reverb. |
| 3 | Hall | 2.2 s (1.4 to 3) | 1 to 3.2 s | Instruments that are plugged in directly have no room sound of their own. |
| 4 | Drum room | 0.8 s (0.4 to 1.2) | 0.3 to 1.2 s | Sampled or close-miked drums can sound small and dry. |

## Ensemble rules

| Rule | Fires when the band has | Arrangement advice (always first) | Changes | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kick and bass share the low end | kick or electronicDrums or cajon AND bassGuitar | Ask the bassist to lock their notes to the kick pattern. Two parts playing together sound like one strong low end. | none | Kick and bass live in the same range. The kick usually owns 60 to 80 Hz and the bass 80 to 120 Hz; neither is cut by default, because the app has not heard them. |
| Keys leave the low notes to the bass guitar | keys AND bassGuitar | Ask the keys player to play the left hand higher, or leave it out, while the bass guitar is playing. | keys: lowCutHz +20 | A piano left hand and a bass guitar in the same octave turn the low end to mud. |
| Keys and electric guitar share the middle | keys AND electricGuitar | Ask the keys and the electric guitar to play in different registers: when one plays low chords, the other plays higher. | none | Both fill the same middle range. Separating them by arrangement works better than any EQ. |
| Acoustic guitar leaves room for the lead vocal | acousticGuitar AND leadVocal | Ask the guitarist to strum more simply during verses so the words stay clear. | none | The body of an acoustic guitar sits right where the vocal's warmth is. |
| Acoustic guitar is rhythm in a full band | acousticGuitar AND bassGuitar AND kick or electronicDrums | With bass and drums playing, the acoustic guitar's job is rhythm and sparkle, not fullness. | acousticGuitar: lowCutHz +20 | In a full band the acoustic guitar's lows only crowd the bass. |
| Pad sits under the keys | padSynth AND keys | Ask the pad player to hold fewer notes, and higher, while the keys are playing chords. | padSynth: lowCutHz +30; padSynth: levelDb -2 | Pad and keys together stack up low-mids quickly. |
| Only one bass instrument owns the low end | synthBass AND bassGuitar | Ask the band to decide, song by song, whether the bass guitar or the synth bass plays the low notes. Not both. | synthBass: lowCutHz +50; synthBass: levelDb -3 No sub send for synthBass. | Two bass sources playing different low notes cancel and boom unpredictably. By default the bass guitar owns the low end. |
