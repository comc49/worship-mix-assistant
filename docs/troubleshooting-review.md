# Troubleshooting review

Generated by `npm run docs` from `src/app/core/knowledge/troubleshooting`. Do not edit by hand: change the data and regenerate.

Names, values and control names below are filled in for the placeholder test band in "Room A (warehouse)" on the Behringer X32. The app fills them in for the current band, room and console.

Every step is one action and one question. Steps marked REHEARSAL ONLY are hidden while the app is set to "Service". Values are typical starting points; ears decide.

## Feedback

### Feedback: low ringing

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: A low ring is a held note that hums or booms and slowly grows. Mute the channel you suspect now. If you do not know which channel it is, mute the open mics one at a time, starting with the one nearest a speaker.
- Ask: Did the ringing stop when a channel was muted?
- Yes: go to step 3. No: go to step 2.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Pull the main fader down 6 dB right away, and the monitor mix masters too if you can reach them. When the ringing has stopped, bring them back slowly and stop about 3 dB below where it started. If it returns, ask for help.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when wedges are on stage. Otherwise go to step 5.
- Do: Find out whether the ring is in a wedge before you touch the main mix. Pull the channel fader all the way down, then unmute the channel. The fader does not change what a pre-fader monitor send feeds to the wedge.
- Ask: Does the ringing come back with the fader down?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask the musician to move the mic or the instrument away from the wedge. An acoustic guitar facing a wedge rings on its own body note.
- Then: The ring is in the wedge, not in the room. Open the sends of that channel (Home screen, sends tab) and turn its send to that monitor mix down 3 dB at a time until the ringing is gone. Then bring the channel fader back up for the room. With wedges, compression on a vocal also reaches the wedge unless the monitor send is taken before the compressor.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: With the channel still muted, look at its low cut (Low Cut) and compare it with your console sheet.
- Ask: Is the low cut off, or set lower than the sheet says?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask the musician to move the mic or the instrument away from the wedge. An acoustic guitar facing a wedge rings on its own body note.
- Then: Switch the low cut on and set it to the value on your console sheet (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)). A low ring often starts below the notes the source really needs. Then unmute and bring the fader up slowly, stopping 3 dB below where it rang.
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutName, lowCutScreen

**7. Check** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Do: Identify the frequency. Bring the fader up slowly until the ringing just starts, and stop there. Open the EQ (Home screen, eq tab), make one band narrow, cut it by 6 dB, and move it slowly through 100 to 300 Hz. Search with the cut. Never turn a band up to find a ring.
- Ask: Did the ringing stop at one spot?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.
- During a service this check is skipped: go to step 10.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask the musician to move the mic or the instrument away from the wedge. An acoustic guitar facing a wedge rings on its own body note.
- Then: Leave the narrow cut at that spot and make it only as deep as it needs to be: try 3 to 4 dB. Write the frequency down, because the same mic in the same place will ring there again.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**9. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask the musician to move the mic or the instrument away from the wedge. An acoustic guitar facing a wedge rings on its own body note.
- Then: No single cut stops it, so the channel is simply too loud for where the mic stands. Take the cut out again, and run the channel 3 dB lower than where it rang.

**10. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask the musician to move the mic or the instrument away from the wedge. An acoustic guitar facing a wedge rings on its own body note.
- Then: Unmute and bring the fader back to about 3 dB below where it rang. If it still rings there, open the EQ (Home screen, eq tab), make one band narrow, cut it by 3 dB at about 200 Hz, and nudge it a little either way until the ring goes. Search with the cut only. Find the exact frequency at the next rehearsal.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

## No sound, noise, distortion

### No sound from a channel

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at the channel you suspect: its mute button, the mute groups (All Band, All Vocals) and the DCA faders it belongs to.
- Ask: Is it muted, or is one of its DCA faders down?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Pull the channel fader down first, then unmute it or bring the DCA fader back to 0. Now bring the channel fader up slowly from the bottom. Do not jump straight to where it was.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Ask the musician to play or sing, and watch the input meter of the channel you suspect.
- Ask: Does the meter move?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 12.

**4. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Signal is arriving. Look at the channel fader.
- Ask: Is the fader all the way down?
- Yes: go to step 5. No: go to step 6.

**5. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Bring the fader up slowly until the source sits in the mix. Stop if you hear ringing.

**6. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Open the gate for that channel (Home screen, gate tab) and watch it while the musician plays.
- Ask: Is a gate switched on and staying closed while they play?
- Yes: go to step 7. No: go to step 8.

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Lower the gate threshold (threshold (1st encoder)) until the gate opens on every note, also the soft ones. If it still chops notes off, switch the gate off: an open channel is better than a missing one.
- Console facts listed with the fix: gateScreen, gateParamNames

**8. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at whether the channel is assigned to the main mix.
- Ask: Is it assigned to the main mix?
- Yes: go to step 11. No: go to step 9.

**9. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Pull the channel fader down, assign the channel to the main mix, then bring the fader up slowly. This is the place where that assignment lives on the Behringer X32: Select the channel, open the Home screen main tab, and tap the 1st encoder so the channel is no longer assigned to the main stereo output (or turn off the "Stereo Bus" button on the channel strip).
- Console facts listed with the fix: removeChannelFromMain
- During a service, shown instead: step 10.

**10. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The channel has signal but is not part of the main mix. Changing where a channel goes is not a safe move while people are listening. Tell the tech lead now, and let them decide.

**11. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The console receives the signal and sends it on, so the problem is after this channel: an output, an amplifier or a speaker. Ask the tech lead.

**12. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: No signal reaches the console. Check the source on stage: the mic switch, the instrument volume, the battery or wireless receiver, and the cable at both ends.
- Ask: Did you find something switched off, flat or unplugged?
- Yes: go to step 13. No: go to step 14.

**13. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Mute the channel before you touch any plug, so nobody hears a bang. Fix what you found, unmute, and bring the fader up slowly.

**14. Check** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Do: Find out whether the source needs phantom power: a condenser mic or an active DI box does. Then look at the phantom power for that input (48 V button in the channel strip Config/Preamp section).
- Ask: Does the source need phantom power while it is switched off?
- Yes: go to step 15. No: go to step 16.
- During a service this check is skipped: go to step 16.

**15. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Mute the channel, switch phantom power on for that input, wait a few seconds, then unmute and bring the fader up slowly.
- Console facts listed with the fix: phantomPower
- During a service, shown instead: "The fix for this needs a change that is not safe while people are listening. For now, get through the service with small fader moves only. Afterwards, switch to Rehearsal and work through this problem again to see the full fix."

**16. Check** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Do: Look at the preamp gain of that input (Gain knob in the channel strip Config/Preamp section).
- Ask: Is the gain turned all the way down?
- Yes: go to step 17. No: go to step 18.
- During a service this check is skipped: go to step 18.

**17. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Pull the fader down, then set the gain the way the gain staging step on your console sheet describes, while the musician plays their loudest. Bring the fader back up slowly.
- Console facts listed with the fix: preampGain
- During a service, shown instead: "The fix for this needs a change that is not safe while people are listening. For now, get through the service with small fader moves only. Afterwards, switch to Rehearsal and work through this problem again to see the full fix."

**18. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Mute the channel, swap the cable for one you know works, and unmute.
- Ask: Does the meter move now?
- Yes: go to step 19. No: go to step 20.

**19. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The old cable is faulty. Put a label on it and take it off the stage, so it does not come back next week. Bring the fader up slowly.

**20. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- A good cable and a working source still give no signal, so the fault is in the mic, the DI box or the input itself. Ask the tech lead. If a spare mic or DI box is at hand, swap it in with the channel muted. Otherwise carry on without this channel for now.

### Hum or buzz

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Mute the channel you suspect. If you are not sure which channel it is, mute the instrument channels one at a time.
- Ask: Did the noise stop when one channel was muted?
- Yes: go to step 5. No: go to step 2.

**2. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Between songs, pull the main fader all the way down for a moment, then put it back.
- Ask: Was the noise still there with the main fader down?
- Yes: go to step 3. No: go to step 4.

**3. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The noise is there with nothing coming out of the console, so it comes from the amplifiers, the speakers or the power they are on. Ask the tech lead.

**4. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The noise comes through the console but not from one channel alone. Several sources may share the same power problem. Keep the instruments that are not playing muted, and ask the tech lead.

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Unmute the channel and ask the musician to turn the volume on their instrument all the way down.
- Ask: Is the noise still there with the instrument volume at zero?
- Yes: go to step 7. No: go to step 6.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The noise starts in the instrument, not in the cabling. Guitar pickups buzz near stage lights, dimmers and screens: ask the musician to turn or step away until it drops. A laptop often hums through its charger: run it on battery. Until it is solved, mute the channel between songs.

**7. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Mute the channel, press the ground lift switch on the DI box, and unmute.
- Ask: Is the hum gone?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Leave the ground lift switch where it is now, and tell the tech lead so it is noted for next week.

**9. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Mute the channel, swap the cable between the instrument, the DI box and the stage for one you know works, and unmute.
- Ask: Is the noise gone?
- Yes: go to step 10. No: go to step 11.

**10. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The old cable is faulty. Label it and take it off the stage.

**11. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The cable and the ground lift did not help, so this is most likely a power problem: the instrument and the sound system are on different circuits, or a power supply is faulty. Ask the tech lead. Until then, mute the channel whenever the instrument is not playing.

### Distortion or clipping

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Watch the input meter of the channel you suspect during the loudest moment.
- Ask: Does the meter reach the red, or does the clip light come on?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Turn the preamp gain of that input down (Gain knob in the channel strip Config/Preamp section) until the loudest moments stay clear of the red. Then bring the channel fader up to get the level back. Turning the gain DOWN on a clipping input is the one gain change that is fine during a service. The monitors get a little quieter too, so look at the musician and be ready for a signal.
- Console facts listed with the fix: preampGain

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Listen to the channel alone on headphones with its solo button. Solo is only for judging the source: it does not change what the room hears.
- Ask: Is it distorted in the headphones too?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The meter is fine but the sound is already distorted, so it is distorting before the console. Ask the musician to turn the output of the instrument or pedal board down a little, or press the pad switch on the DI box. On a wireless mic, check the battery. Mute the channel while anything is unplugged.

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Open the compressor of that channel (Home screen, dyn tab) and watch its reduction meter in the loud moments.
- Ask: Does it show more than about 10 dB of reduction, or can you hear the sound pumping?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The compressor is working too hard. Bring the threshold (threshold (1st encoder)) back up until the loudest moments show only a few dB of reduction, as on your console sheet.
- Console facts listed with the fix: compScreen, compParamNames

**7. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Watch the main meters during the loudest moment.
- Ask: Do the main meters reach the red?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The whole mix is too hot for the main bus. Pull every DCA fader down by the same 3 dB, slowly, so the balance stays the same. Do not fix this with the limiter.

**9. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- Nothing on the console is clipping, so an amplifier or a speaker is being overloaded or is damaged. Pull the main fader down 3 dB to protect it, and ask the tech lead.

## The whole mix

### Mix is muddy

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the room is live. Otherwise go to step 5.
- Do: This room builds up low-mids by itself, so count what is playing at once. Pull the Keys and Pads DCA down 5 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Does the mix clear up?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: In a reverberant room, fewer simultaneous sources beats lower faders. Ask the pad player to hold fewer notes, and higher, while the keys are playing chords. Ask the keys player to play the left hand higher, or leave it out, while the bass guitar is playing.
- Then: Until the band can change the arrangement, leave the Keys and Pads DCA 2 to 3 dB lower than it was.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put the DCA back. Open the EQ (Home screen, eq tab) of the keys, then the pad, then the acoustic guitar, and on each one try the optional low-mid cut from your console sheet: 2 to 3 dB, wide, around 250 to 300 Hz. Listen after each one.
- Ask: Is the mix clearer?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Keep the cuts that made a difference and take out the ones that did not. Do not cut the same frequency on every channel: that only makes the whole mix thin.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at the low cut (Low Cut) on every channel except the kick and the bass, and compare with your console sheet.
- Ask: Is a low cut switched off, or set clearly lower than the sheet says?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Set those low cuts to the values on your console sheet (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)). Low cuts keep rumble from every mic and instrument out of the low end, where it adds up to mud.
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutName, lowCutScreen

**7. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Pull the bass fader down 5 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Does the mud clear?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the bassist to lock their notes to the kick pattern. Two parts playing together sound like one strong low end.
- Then: The bass is the cause. Leave it 2 dB lower for now, then work through "Bass is boomy".

**9. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the band has this role: keys. Otherwise go to step 11.
- Do: Put the bass back. Pull the Keys and Pads DCA down 5 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Does the mud clear?
- Yes: go to step 10. No: go to step 11.

**10. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the keys player to play the left hand higher, or leave it out, while the bass guitar is playing.
- Then: Leave the Keys and Pads DCA 2 dB lower, and try the optional low-mid cut from your console sheet on the keys: 2 dB, wide, around 300 Hz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**11. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put everything back. Pull the FX Returns DCA all the way down for a moment.
- Ask: Does the mix clear up?
- Yes: go to step 12. No: go to step 13.

**12. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Bring the FX Returns DCA back up only as far as you need: start 6 dB lower than it was. Reverb that you can clearly hear in a full mix is usually too much.

**13. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Arrangement beats EQ. If two sources fight, first ask who should play less, before any fader or EQ move.
- Then: Open the EQ (Home screen, eq tab) and try the optional low-mid cuts from your console sheet, one channel at a time: 2 to 3 dB, wide, around 250 to 300 Hz, on keys, pad and acoustic guitar. Keep only the cuts you can hear helping.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

### Mix is harsh or painful

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Pull the Energy DCA down 3 dB. It holds the cymbals, the snare and the electric guitar.
- Ask: Is the pain gone?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: If the cymbals or the electric guitar are loud on stage as well, ask the player to play them more gently. The console cannot turn down what the room hears directly.
- Then: Leave the Energy DCA where it is now. Ride it up only for the big moments, and bring it back afterwards.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put the Energy DCA back. Pull the Lead Vocal and Harmony Vocals DCAs down 3 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Is the pain gone?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The vocals carry the harshness. Put the DCAs back, and on the loudest voice cut 2 dB, medium width, around 3 kHz (Home screen, eq tab). If that is not enough, work through "Vocals harsh or sibilant".
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the band has this role: keys. Otherwise go to step 7.
- Do: Put the vocals back. Pull the Keys and Pads DCA down 3 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Is the pain gone?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask Keys to choose a softer piano sound, or to play the right hand an octave lower.
- Then: Leave the Keys and Pads DCA 2 dB lower, and on the keys cut 2 dB, wide, around 2 to 3 kHz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: No single group is the cause, so it is the overall level: ears hear a mix that is too loud as harsh. Pull every DCA down by the same 2 to 3 dB, slowly. Flat. Do not touch the system EQ: The system EQ was set for the room and the speakers. Mix problems are fixed on channels.

### Mix is thin

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the room is dry. Otherwise go to step 5.
- Do: This room adds no warmth of its own, so look at the low cuts first. Compare the low cut (Low Cut) on the vocals, guitars and keys with your console sheet.
- Ask: Is a low cut set higher than the sheet says?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Bring those low cuts back down to the values on your console sheet (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)). A low cut that is too high removes the body of the source.
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutName, lowCutScreen

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at the FX Returns DCA and the All FX mute group.
- Ask: Is the FX Returns DCA down, or is All FX muted?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Unmute All FX and bring the FX Returns DCA up to 0 while the band plays. In a dry room the reverb is what makes voices and instruments sound full. Stop when you can just tell it is there.
- Console facts listed with the fix: fxScreen

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Push the Low End DCA up 3 dB for a moment. It holds the kick and the bass.
- Ask: Does the mix fill out?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Leave the Low End DCA 2 to 3 dB higher. Establish kick and bass first. Do not high-pass and thin everything out before the low-end foundation is solid.

**7. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the subwoofer has its own feed. Otherwise go to step 9.
- Do: Put the DCA back. The subwoofer has its own feed, so look at the master fader and the mute of that feed.
- Ask: Is the subwoofer feed down or muted?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Unmute the subwoofer feed and bring its master up slowly while the kick and bass play, until the low end is felt but not booming.

**9. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Walk to the subwoofer while the kick and the bass play.
- Ask: Is sound coming out of the subwoofer?
- Yes: go to step 11. No: go to step 10.

**10. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The subwoofer is silent while the kick and bass play. That is a problem with its power, its amplifier or its feed, not with your mix. Ask the tech lead.

**11. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Open the EQ (Home screen, eq tab) of the kick and of the bass and look below 200 Hz. Look at their low cuts too.
- Ask: Is there a deep cut below 200 Hz, or a low cut above the sheet value?
- Yes: go to step 12. No: go to step 13.

**12. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Take those cuts out and set the low cuts back to the sheet values. The bass low cut on your sheet is 35 Hz.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen, lowCutScreen

**13. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask Keys or Pad to hold fuller chords in the quiet sections. A thin arrangement cannot be mixed full.
- Then: The low end is working, so the middle is empty. Push the Keys and Pads DCA up 2 dB, and add a little reverb with the FX Returns DCA.

### Mix is too loud overall

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Between songs or in a quiet moment, pull the main fader down 6 dB for a few seconds, then put it back.
- Ask: Did the room get clearly quieter?
- Yes: go to step 3. No: go to step 2.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: The loudness comes from the stage: wedges, amplifiers or acoustic drums. Ask the band to play more quietly, and offer to turn the wedges down together with them.
- Then: Pull each monitor mix master down 2 to 3 dB, and tell the band you did. Leave the main mix where it is: turning it down would only leave the stage sound on its own.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Listen for one thing that sticks out above everything else.
- Ask: Is one source much louder than the rest?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Pull that one channel or its DCA down 3 dB, and leave the rest alone. A mix often feels too loud because of one source.

**5. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Pull every DCA down by the same 2 to 3 dB, slowly, over a few seconds, so nobody notices a jump. Aim for the level your church is comfortable with. Set the ceiling first: set fader positions at the loudest moment of the loudest song, then work backward.

### Chorus does not lift

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Watch the main bus during a chorus: its compressor and its limiter meter. On the Behringer X32 the main bus has: Insert point, 6-band parametric EQ, compressor/expander, sends to 6 matrices. No built-in limiter is documented.
- Ask: Is the main bus compressor on, or does the limiter show more than 2 dB of reduction in the chorus?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 4.

**2. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Main bus compressor: Off. Over-compressing the main bus flattens builds: the chorus cannot get bigger than the verse. Limiter: On, set so it only catches accidents. It should show no reduction in normal playing, and only 1 to 2 dB at the very loudest moment.
- Console facts listed with the fix: mainBusProcessing
- During a service, shown instead: step 3.

**3. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Switching main bus processing during a service makes the level jump, so leave it for now. Pull every DCA down by the same 2 dB so the mix sits under the limiter, then ride the Low End and Energy DCAs up into the chorus. Change the main bus processing at the next rehearsal.

**4. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at your faders and DCAs during a verse.
- Ask: Is the verse already almost as loud as the chorus?
- Yes: go to step 5. No: go to step 6.

**5. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the band to play less in the verse: a chorus only lifts when the verse leaves room for it.
- Then: The faders started too high. In the verse, pull the Energy DCA and the Keys and Pads DCA down 3 dB, and keep the vocal where it is. Set the ceiling first: set fader positions at the loudest moment of the loudest song, then work backward.

**6. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: At the next chorus, push the Low End DCA and the Energy DCA up 2 to 3 dB together.
- Ask: Does the chorus feel bigger?
- Yes: go to step 7. No: go to step 8.

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Do that at every chorus, and bring both DCAs back down for the verse. Ride up into a climax with the Low End and Energy DCAs instead of pushing everything. A channel that sits in two DCAs moves twice, so keep the moves small.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: The lift has to come from the band. Ask the drummer to open up to the cymbals in the chorus, Electric guitar to play fuller chords, and everyone to hold back in the verse.
- Then: Keep the 2 dB push on the Low End and Energy DCAs in the chorus, and push the Lead Vocal DCA 1 dB with them so the words stay on top.

- Ask: Can you hear the words now?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the guitarist to strum more simply during verses so the words stay clear.
- Then: Leave the Guitars DCA where it is now during the verses, and bring it back up between the sung lines if the song needs it.

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put the guitars back. Push the Lead Vocal DCA up 2 dB, and listen for the first hint of ringing.
- Ask: Are the words clear now, with no ringing?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Keep it there, and keep a finger on the Lead Vocal DCA for the whole service: it is the one fader to ride. Push it for quiet lines and ease it back for loud ones.

**7. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Bring the DCA back if it started to ring. Watch Vocalist A sing.
- Ask: Is the mic further than a hand width from the mouth, or pointing away from it?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask Vocalist A to hold the mic closer, pointing straight at the mouth. A closer mic gives much more voice before feedback starts.
- Then: Until then, push the Lead Vocal DCA only as far as it goes without ringing, and pull the Keys and Pads DCA and the Guitars DCA down 2 dB each.

**9. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Make room where the words are. On the keys and on the electric guitar, cut 2 dB, wide, around 3 kHz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Ask: Are the words clearer?
- Yes: go to step 10. No: go to step 11.

**10. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Keep those cuts. Cutting the instruments where the voice needs to be heard is safer than turning that range up on a live mic.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**11. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Take the cuts out again, and look at the lead vocal compressor (Home screen, dyn tab). If it shows much more than 4 dB of reduction on loud lines, the voice is being flattened: bring the threshold (threshold (1st encoder)) back up until it shows about that much. If it shows none at all, quiet lines get lost: lower the threshold until loud lines show about that much.
- Console facts listed with the fix: compScreen, compParamNames

### Vocals harsh or sibilant

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Ask: Is it mainly the sharp "s" and "t" sounds?

- Yes: go to step 3. No: go to step 4.

- Then: Turn the de-esser up a little at a time. Sharp "s" sounds soften. If the singer starts to lisp, it is doing too much.

**4. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Insert the de-esser on the lead vocal as your console sheet describes. On the Behringer X32: On the Effects screen, set both inputs of the slot to "Insert" and pick the effect. Then select the channel, open the Home screen config tab, choose the slot with the 6th encoder and press to connect. Compression and bright mics make "s" sounds sharp. - During a service, shown instead: step 5.

- With the band first: Between songs, ask Vocalist A to sing slightly across the mic instead of straight into it.

**6. Check** (safe during a service)

- Ask: Is the harshness gone, with the words still clear?
- Yes: go to step 7. No: go to step 8.

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Leave it there. A voice that is a little too loud is heard as harsh.

**8. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put the fader back. On that vocal, cut 2 to 3 dB, medium width, around 3 kHz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Ask: Is it easier to listen to?
- Yes: go to step 9. No: go to step 10.

**9. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Keep the cut, and make it only as deep as it needs to be. Check on a loud line that the words are still clear.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**10. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Take the cut out, and look at the compressor on that vocal (Home screen, dyn tab). Heavy compression makes a voice hard and brings the "s" sounds forward. Bring the threshold (threshold (1st encoder)) back up until loud lines show about 4 dB of reduction. With wedges, compression on a vocal also reaches the wedge unless the monitor send is taken before the compressor.
- Console facts listed with the fix: compScreen, compParamNames

**12. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Pull the bass fader down 3 dB.
- Ask: Is the boom gone, with the bass still heard?
- Yes: go to step 13. No: go to step 14.

- Then: Leave the bass there. It was simply too loud.

- Ask: Does it show about 2 dB of reduction on the loud parts?
- Yes: go to step 9. No: go to step 8.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask Electric guitar to match the levels of their sounds, so the lead sound is only a little louder than the rhythm sound.
- Then: Lower the compressor threshold (threshold (1st encoder)) until the loud parts show about 2 dB of reduction.
- Console facts listed with the fix: compScreen, compParamNames

**9. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask Electric guitar to match the levels of their sounds, so the lead sound is only a little louder than the rhythm sound.
- Then: The compressor is doing its part, so the jumps are too big for it. Keep a finger on the guitar fader and pull it back 3 dB when the loud sound comes in.

### Electric guitar too thin

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at the low cut (Low Cut) on the electric guitar.
- Ask: Is it set clearly higher than 110 Hz?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Bring the low cut back to 110 Hz (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)), the value on your console sheet.
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutName, lowCutScreen

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Listen to the guitar alone on headphones with its solo button. Solo is only for judging the source: it does not change what the room hears.
- Ask: Is it thin in the headphones too?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: The sound is thin before it reaches the console. Ask Electric guitar to check that the speaker cabinet simulation is switched on in their sound, and to choose a fuller one. A direct guitar signal without it sounds thin and fizzy.
- Then: No EQ on the console can add what the source does not send. Until the sound is changed, lift 2 dB, wide, around 200 to 250 Hz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Open the guitar EQ (Home screen, eq tab) and look between 150 and 500 Hz.
- Ask: Is there a cut deeper than 3 dB in that range?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Make that cut shallower, 1 dB at a time, until the guitar has body again.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the keys and the electric guitar to play in different registers: when one plays low chords, the other plays higher.
- Then: The guitar is fine alone but thin in the mix, so something else is filling its range. Push the guitar 1 to 2 dB and lift 2 dB, wide, around 200 to 250 Hz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

### Electric guitar too harsh

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Pull the electric guitar fader down 3 dB.
- Ask: Is the harshness gone, with the guitar still heard?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Leave it there. It was a level problem, not a tone problem.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put the fader back. Listen to the guitar alone on headphones with its solo button. Solo is only for judging the source: it does not change what the room hears.
- Ask: Is it harsh or fizzy in the headphones too?
- Yes: go to step 5. No: go to step 4.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The source is fine, so the harshness comes from level in the room. Pull the Energy DCA down 2 dB: the guitar and the cymbals add up in the same range.

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: On the electric guitar, cut 3 dB, wide, around 3 kHz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Ask: Is it easier to listen to?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Between songs, ask Electric guitar to turn the treble or presence of their sound down a little, so the fix is made at the source.
- Then: Keep the cut, only as deep as it needs to be.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask Electric guitar to use less distortion and less treble in their sound, and to check that the speaker cabinet simulation is switched on.
- Then: Keep the 3 kHz cut, and turn down everything above 6 kHz by 3 dB with a high shelf (Home screen, eq tab). Fizz lives up there, and a guitar needs very little of it.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

### Keys fight the guitars

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Pull the keys fader down 4 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Can you hear the guitar part clearly now?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 3.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the keys and the electric guitar to play in different registers: when one plays low chords, the other plays higher.
- Then: Leave the keys 2 dB lower than they were. If they still cover the guitar, cut 2 dB, wide, around 500 Hz on the keys (Home screen, eq tab), so the guitar has the middle.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the band has this role: padSynth. Otherwise go to step 5.
- Do: Put the keys back. Pull the pad fader down 5 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Are the keys and the guitar easier to tell apart now?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the pad player to hold fewer notes, and higher, while the keys are playing chords.
- Then: The pad is filling the space between them. Leave it 3 dB lower: a pad should be missed when it is muted, not heard when it plays.

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Put everything back. On the keys, cut 2 to 3 dB, wide, around 300 Hz (Home screen, eq tab).
- Ask: Are the two easier to tell apart?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the keys and the electric guitar to play in different registers: when one plays low chords, the other plays higher.
- Then: Keep the cut on the keys. It is the optional low-mid cut from your console sheet.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Arrangement beats EQ. If two sources fight, first ask who should play less, before any fader or EQ move. Ask the keys and the electric guitar to play in different registers: when one plays low chords, the other plays higher.
- Then: Take the cut out. Faders and EQ cannot separate two parts that play the same notes in the same rhythm. Until the band changes the parts, choose one to lead in each song and run the other 3 dB lower.

### Keys fight the bass

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at the low cut (Low Cut) on the keys.
- Ask: Is it on, and close to 130 Hz?
- Yes: go to step 3. No: go to step 2.

**2. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the keys player to play the left hand higher, or leave it out, while the bass guitar is playing.
- Then: Switch the keys low cut on and set it to 130 Hz (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)). It keeps the left hand out of the range the bass needs.
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutName, lowCutScreen

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Pull the keys fader down 4 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Is the bass line clear now?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the keys player to play the left hand higher, or leave it out, while the bass guitar is playing.
- Then: Leave the keys 2 dB lower, and raise the keys low cut by 20 Hz, staying inside the range on your console sheet (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)).
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutScreen

**5. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when the band has this role: padSynth. Otherwise go to step 7.
- Do: Put the keys back. Pull the pad fader down 5 dB for a moment.
- Ask: Is the bass line clear now?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 7.

**6. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the pad player to hold fewer notes, and higher, while the keys are playing chords.
- Then: The pad is holding low notes under the bass. Leave it 3 dB lower and check that its low cut is on and near 170 Hz (Home screen, config tab (2nd encoder)).
- Console facts listed with the fix: lowCutScreen

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the band to decide, song by song, whether the bass guitar or the synth bass plays the low notes. Not both. Ask the keys player to play the left hand higher, or leave it out, while the bass guitar is playing.
- Then: Put everything back. On the keys, cut 3 dB, wide, around 100 to 200 Hz (Home screen, eq tab), so only the bass plays down there.
- Console facts listed with the fix: eqScreen

## Monitors and stream

### A musician cannot hear themselves

Starts at step 1.

**1. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Ask the musician what exactly is missing: only themselves, or everything.
- Ask: Is their whole monitor silent or very quiet?
- Yes: go to step 2. No: go to step 7.

**2. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at the master of their monitor mix: its fader and its mute.
- Ask: Is it down or muted?
- Yes: go to step 3. No: go to step 4.

**3. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Unmute it and bring it up slowly while the musician signals to you. Stop at the first hint of ringing.

**4. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Check their end on stage: the wedge is powered and plugged in, or the in-ear pack is switched on, turned up and has a good battery.
- Ask: Did you find something off, flat or unplugged?
- Yes: go to step 5. No: go to step 6.

**5. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Pull the master of that monitor mix down before you plug anything in or switch it on. Fix what you found, then bring the master up slowly.

**6. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The mix is up on the console and the monitor is switched on, but nothing arrives. The fault is in between: an output, a cable or an amplifier. Ask the tech lead.

**7. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Select the channel you suspect and look at its send to their monitor mix (Home screen, sends tab).
- Ask: Is that send down or switched off?
- Yes: go to step 8. No: go to step 9.

**8. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Bring that send up 2 to 3 dB at a time while the musician signals to you. Your console sheet starts a musician's own channel at 0 dB in their own mix.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**9. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Look at where that send is taken from. For a monitor it should be: Pre-Fader.
- Ask: Is it set that way?
- Yes: go to step 12. No: go to step 10.

**10. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Set that send to: Pre-Fader. Monitor sends are pre-fader, so moving a channel fader for the room does not change what the musician hears.
- Console facts listed with the fix: monitorSendTap, sendsScreen
- During a service, shown instead: step 11.

**11. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Right now their monitor follows your channel fader: every move you make for the room changes what they hear. Changing that is not safe while people are listening. For now, bring the send up to make up for it (Home screen, sends tab), and avoid big fader moves on that channel. Correct it at the next rehearsal.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**12. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Turn something else down before you turn them up. In their monitor mix, turn the loudest other instrument down 3 dB.
- Ask: Can they hear themselves now?
- Yes: go to step 13. No: go to step 14.

**13. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Leave it there. Taking things out of a monitor mix keeps the stage quieter than adding more, and a quieter stage makes the room mix clearer too.

**14. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Only when wedges are on stage. Otherwise go to step 15.
- With the band first: Ask the musician to stand closer to their wedge, and turn the wedge so it points at their ears and not at their knees.
- Then: Raise their own send 2 dB at a time, and stop at the first hint of ringing. Wedges are in use. The compressed vocal reaches the wedges, and every dB of compression lowers the level at which feedback starts, so vocal gain reduction is capped at 6 dB.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**15. Fix** (safe during a service)

- With the band first: Ask the musician to check that both earpieces seal well. An earpiece that does not seal loses its low end and makes everything sound far away.
- Then: Raise their own send 2 to 3 dB at a time until they signal that it is enough. In-ears do not feed back into the mics, so the vocal compression cap is lifted.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

### Stream mix sounds different from the room

Starts at step 2.

**1. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Your setup has no separate stream mix, so the stream gets a copy of the room mix. It will sound different: The room adds the congregation's singing and the direct sound of the stage. The stream has neither. Nothing on the console fixes that safely right now. To change it, switch on the separate stream mix on the Setup screen and build it at a rehearsal.

**2. Check** (safe during a service)

- Only when a separate stream mix is in use. Otherwise go to step 1.
- Do: Listen to the stream mix on headphones. Check the stream on headphones, never by listening to the room: What you hear in the room is not what the stream gets.
- Ask: Is every voice and instrument there in the stream mix?
- Yes: go to step 6. No: go to step 3.

**3. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Select the channel that is missing and look at its send to the stream mix (Home screen, sends tab).
- Ask: Is that send down or switched off?
- Yes: go to step 4. No: go to step 5.

**4. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: Bring that send up to 0 dB. Start the stream mix from post-fader sends at 0 dB, so it follows your room mix. The stream then needs only corrections, not a second full mix.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**5. Ask for help** (safe during a service)

- The send is up but the channel does not reach the stream. Ask the tech lead, or whoever runs the stream, to check what the stream receives.

**6. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Listen to the words on the stream. Aim for: Every word clear on a phone speaker.
- Ask: Are the vocals too quiet on the stream?
- Yes: go to step 7. No: go to step 8.

**7. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: In the stream mix: Vocals 2 to 3 dB higher than in the room. The room adds the congregation's singing and the direct sound of the stage. The stream has neither. Raise the vocal sends to the stream (Home screen, sends tab), not the channel faders.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**8. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Listen to the space around the voices on the stream.
- Ask: Does the stream sound dry, like a small closed room?
- Yes: go to step 9. No: go to step 10.

**9. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: In the stream mix: More reverb than in the room. The stream has no room sound, so a mix that is right in the room sounds dry online. Raise the sends from the effect returns to the stream mix a little at a time. Voices should not sound like they are in a closet.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen, fxScreen

**10. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Listen for the sources that are loud on stage: drums, cymbals and guitar amplifiers.
- Ask: Are they too quiet on the stream?
- Yes: go to step 11. No: go to step 12.

**11. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: In the stream mix: Acoustic drums, cymbals and guitar amps higher than in the room. Loud stage sources are turned down in the room mix because the room already hears them directly. On headphones the kit should sound complete.
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen

**12. Check** (safe during a service)

- Do: Move one channel fader for the room and listen to the stream.
- Ask: Does the stream follow your fader move?
- Yes: go to step 15. No: go to step 13.

**13. Fix** (REHEARSAL ONLY)

- Then: Start the stream mix from post-fader sends at 0 dB, so it follows your room mix. The stream then needs only corrections, not a second full mix. Set the stream sends to post-fader on the sends screen (Home screen, sends tab).
- Console facts listed with the fix: sendsScreen
- During a service, shown instead: step 14.

**14. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The stream does not follow your room mix, and changing that is not safe while people are listening. For now, after every big move for the room, check the stream on headphones and make the same move on its send. Correct it at the next rehearsal.

**15. Fix** (safe during a service)

- Then: The stream mix is complete and follows the room. It is meant to sound a little different. Keep checking it on headphones. Listen for: Balance and loudness between songs and speech.
